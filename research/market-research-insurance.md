## 1. Licensing Reality Check

**How non-resident licensing works:** Under GLBA and the NAIC Producer Licensing Model Act, a producer needs an active license in every state where they solicit/sell/service policies. A "resident" license is issued by your home/domicile state; a "non-resident" license is issued by any other state, via reciprocity, if lines of authority match (California is the main friction point — sometimes requires its own exam). Applications flow through NIPR (National Insurance Producer Registry); processing 3–14 business days, fees ~$15–$100/state. [nipr.com/licensing-center/state-requirements](https://nipr.com/licensing-center/state-requirements)

The agency does **not** hold a "master license" that substitutes for the individual's — both the business entity and the individual generally need licenses/appointments per state. What varies is who pays for/administers adding the individual to additional states.

**TX/FL-specific issue, personal to Yaser:** Texas requires a *resident* license applicant to actually reside in Texas. Per TDI: *"If you've moved from Texas to another state, you must get a resident license in your new state before TDI can change your Texas resident license to a nonresident license."* **Yaser should verify directly with TDI** whether his TX license is resident or non-resident, given his FL mailing address — unresolved, could be a compliance gap. [TDI FIN594](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/forms/finagentlicense/fin594.pdf)

**Can a TX-only-licensed, FL-resident agent work fully remote today?**
- Yes, if selling only to TX-domiciled customers (TX-based agency/MGA, or a carrier's TX-licensed book/pod).
- Conditionally yes, if the employer sponsors non-resident licenses as leads dictate (common at larger captives/InsurTechs).
- No/limited, if the role requires already holding many states' licenses on day one.

## 4. Compensation & realism (2026)
- BLS median, insurance sales agents overall: $62,280/yr median, $81,510/yr mean (May 2025).
- Remote Personal Lines Account Manager: ~$45k–$95k range depending on employer size; larger brokers (e.g. HUB-tier) trend $61k–$80k base, $93k–$103k total comp.
- Realistic Year-1 remote target: **$45,000–$70,000 base**, total comp up to $70–95k+ at larger brokers with bonus/renewal structure. Six-figure remote producer comp is achievable but usually needs heavy commission exposure or 3+ years book-building — not a safe Year-1 assumption.
- Industry-wide: insurance job openings fell from ~281k avg to ~138k by Dec 2025 (Jacobson Group/Aon Q1 2026 study); P&C headcount grew only 0.81% vs 1.42% forecast — a tightening, more selective hiring market even as remote expectations rise.
- Structural tailwind: ~400,000 industry retirements projected 2021–2026 with a thin pipeline — supports remote flexibility as a retention/recruiting lever long-term.

## 5. Watch-outs
- **"Remote" that's actually residency-locked** — The Zebra example; always read requirements, not just the title, for "must reside in / must relocate to."
- **"Remote" excluding specific states** (CA/NY/AK/HI/WA common exclusions) — confirm FL isn't inadvertently excluded.
- **Unresolved TX license residency status** — verify with TDI before leaning hard on "TX-licensed, remote anywhere" as the pitch.
- **1099 commission-only vs W2** — many "unlimited earning potential" listings are 1099, no benefits; need ~25-40% more pay than W2 to break even after self-employment tax. Screen hard for W2 base+bonus unless deliberately chasing commission upside.
- **Licensing-cost cost-shifting** — ask upfront whether the employer pays for non-resident licenses/E&O/CE (Allstate, Assurance IQ do explicitly; many smaller agencies don't).
- **Quota/dialer-style captive roles** (e.g. "15 apps/week" cold-call quotas) are outbound-hunter jobs, different day-to-day than the book-management/service role at BrokerLink — distinguish "new business production" vs "book management/renewals/service" in postings.
